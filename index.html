The eight rules

Name the binding constraint first.
Growth is limited by one thing at a time: acquisition cost, conversion, retention, reach or capacity. Solve that, then diagnose again.
Optimise to monetised outcomes, not leads.
Tune campaigns toward the deepest event you can measure reliably, and judge every channel on cost per that outcome.
Scale in steps and hold the line.
Agree a cost tolerance before adding budget. A dip is a reason to optimise unless a pre-agreed red flag is crossed.
Never depend on one channel.
Cap any single channel or campaign at about half of acquisition, and build a second and third source before you need them.
Pair paid with earned.
Paid buys speed. Brand, search, content and partnerships build organic pull that lowers blended cost over time.
Fix the leaks before adding water.
Friction in sign-up, pricing, payment and onboarding wastes every unit of traffic you buy.
Reach before message.
Lifecycle messages work only as far as the channel reaches the customer. Measure deliverability and opt-in before rewriting copy.
One owner, one number, one cadence.
Every channel and metric has a named owner, a single definition, and a fixed review rhythm.

The ten-step playbook

Run the steps in order for a new engagement. Each step ends in an output that feeds the next.

  1. DiagnosePull 6 to 12 months of funnel, cohort and channel data. Separate steady-state performance from scale-up effects.Output: a one-page diagnosis naming the constraint.
  2. Set goals and guardrailsSet a headline goal, a cost ceiling, a quality floor and a retention target.Output: a goal tree with thresholds.
  3. Segment and positionDefine priority segments by need and value. Write one clear promise per product and remove overlaps.Output: a segment map and a positioning statement per product.
  4. Build the funnel modelDefine stages, conversion rates and unit costs, then model spend to outcome by month.Output: a monthly projection.
  5. Design the channel mixGive each channel a role, a target share and a concentration cap. Add new channels in a planned order.Output: a channel plan with owners and start dates.
  6. Build brand and organic pullPlan always-on awareness, search presence, content, partnerships and referrals with a share-of-voice target.Output: a brand and organic plan with a budget share.
  7. Remove conversion frictionAudit sign-up, pricing, payment and onboarding. Rank fixes by impact and effort, and test the biggest drops.Output: a prioritised fix list, P0 to P2.
  8. Design lifecycle and retentionMap onboarding, activation, repeat use and win-back. Check reach per channel and set one message cadence.Output: journeys and a retention target.
  9. Activate partners and offlineGive intermediaries leads, co-branded assets and tracking. Track every lead to closure.Output: a partner and offline playbook.
  10. Govern and measureBuild the KPI tree, assign owners, and set review cadence and a risk register.Output: an owner matrix and a dashboard.

Two worked examples

The same playbook, applied to two very different businesses. Figures are illustrative round numbers created for teaching, not data from any company.

Investing platform

A digital-first broker with a large app base. It buys leads through search and social, opens accounts, and earns when customers place their first trade. Costs per account look acceptable, but customers who never trade leave, and the active base is shrinking.

Binding constraint: cost per first-trade customer rises whenever spend rises.

Airline

A full-service carrier that sells through its own site and app, but depends on online travel agents and metasearch for a large share of bookings, at a commission. Demand is seasonal and route-specific, and loyalty enrolment at booking is low.

Binding constraint: direct share of bookings is too low, so distribution cost stays high.

In both cases the move is the same: choose the constraint, set guardrails, shift optimisation toward the deepest outcome, then widen the channel base.

Funnel formulas

Write the funnel as stages with a single conversion rate between each. Judge every channel on cost per the deepest reliable outcome.

Cost per lead = spend / leads
CAC = spend / new customers
Cost per monetised customer = cost per lead / (lead-to-customer rate x customer-to-monetised rate)
Blended CAC = (paid spend + other acquisition cost) / all new customers, paid and organic
Payback (months) = CAC / monthly margin per customer
LTV to CAC = lifetime margin / CAC

Set these guardrails before scaling: a cost tolerance (for example, up to 20% above today), a quality floor for wrong-fit leads, a numeric red-flag trigger for pausing, and one fixed definition for each conversion window (same month, day 7, day 30, rolling).

Scale-up playbook

A budget increase of about 40% exposes problems that steady-state spend hides: weaker lead quality, slower follow-up, lower conversion. Plan for it, and do not let it trigger a retreat. The funnel lab above shows the pattern: the naive path pushes cost per customer up, while optimising deeper lets cost per lead rise but brings cost per customer down.

  1. Baseline in steady stateChange nothing about targeting, follow-up, journeys or onboarding, so you can see what volume alone does.
  2. Fix capacity before volumeCheck call connectivity, follow-up time, verification and message reach.
  3. Step the budgetRaise spend in tranches and read results at a fixed point before the next step.
  4. Move optimisation deeperShift campaign signals from top-funnel to mid- and bottom-funnel events.
  5. Expect cost per lead to riseJudge on cost per monetised customer instead.
  6. Reallocate, do not just cutRun old and new campaigns side by side, then move budget on evidence.
  7. Use red flags, not instinctPause only when a pre-agreed threshold is breached for a full cycle.
  8. Protect the windowClear compliance and platform approvals before launch. One roadblock can pause every campaign.

Conversion, lifecycle and retention

Conversion friction audit

  • Find the stage with the largest drop and test it first.
  • Add trust signals: testimonials, a calculator, clear credentials, and the reasoning behind each recommendation.
  • Remove price disparity and offer clutter that blurs the core value.
  • Let customers buy before completing non-essential verification, then collect it afterward.
  • Offer every payment option on every plan, and make recurring-payment set-up amounts match what the customer sees.
  • Rename complex product names in plain words, and add two or three short onboarding screens.

Lifecycle communication

  • Measure reach by channel before editing any message, and fix infrastructure until reach passes your target (80% is a reasonable goal).
  • Replace offer-led messages with product-led ones that explain use.
  • Bundle messages into one notification centre or cadence.
  • Track conversion by day 7, 15 and 30 for every journey.
  • Feed call and event data back to the customer data platform so segments improve.

Retention and revenue per customer

  • Drive a first transaction before pushing the second product.
  • Raise products per customer with recommendations by affluence or need.
  • Add non-transactional reasons to return: content, watchlists, alerts, trackers.
  • Connect advisors or sales teams to digital journeys.
  • Build an older-customer pool and reactivate it, because a large share of new monetised customers often come from earlier leads.

  • Keep a risk register with owners for compliance, platform dependence, vendor migration and data quality.
